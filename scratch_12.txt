SECTION 2 The Simple Idea: Create a New Featurex2
▷HOOK
Do not think of Polynomial Regression as a completely new algorithm. Think of it as afeature
transformation: creating a new input column from an existing input column.
For example:
•If the original feature value isx= 4, the new feature isx2 = 16.
•Ifx= 8, thenx 2 = 64.
Notice thatx 2 grows faster whenxis large. This gives the model a way tobend upward.

SECTION 4 Generalizing to Higher-Degree Polynomial Features
Apolynomial degreeis the highest power ofxused in the model:
•Degree 1:y=β 0 +β 1x(straight line)
•Degree 2:y=β 0 +β 1x+β 2x2 (can create a U-shape or upward bend)
•Degree 3:y=β 0 +β 1x+β 2x2 +β 3x3 (can bend more flexibly)
The general degree-npolynomial regression model is:
y=β 0 +β 1x+β 2x2 +···+β nxn

△WARNING — OVERFITTING
Overfittingmeans the model captures accidental fluctuations in the training data instead of the
stable pattern that will repeat on future data. A very high-degree polynomial can twist itself to
reduce training error, but that does not guarantee better prediction on new students.
Classroom question:If a degree-8 curve passes closer to every training point than a degree-2
curve, should we automatically prefer degree 8?
Expected answer:No. Lower training error is not enough. The curve must represent the
underlying relationship, not thenoise in one dataset.

PART II: THE FIVE ASSUMPTIONS OF LINEAR REGRESSION
WHY ASSUMPTIONS MATTER
Polynomial Regression showed us an important lesson: Linear Regression is powerful when the
representation of the data matches the real pattern. But after choosing a model, we still need to
ask whether the model isbehaving in a trustworthy way.
Linear Regression relies on several assumptions to ensure the validity and reliability of the
estimates and inferences. Here,inferencemeans understanding the relationship between
variables, interpreting coefficients, and making statements about how changes in inputs affect
the output.
Notation:
•Residual= actual value−predicted value:e=y−ˆy
The five key assumptions:
1. Linearity
2. Normality of Residuals
3. Homoscedasticity
4. No Autocorrelation of Errors
5. No (or little) Multicollinearity
SECTION 5 Assumption 1 — Linearity
▷HOOK
The assumption:There is alinear relationshipbetween the independent variables and
the dependent variable. The model assumes that changes in the independent variables lead to
proportional changes in the dependent variable.

Linearity means the central trend should be well represented by a straight line.
A curved pattern suggests violation of the linearity assumption.
Linearity doesnotmean every point must lie exactly on a line. It means thecentral trendshould
be reasonably represented by a straight line or by a linear combination of features.
How to check this assumption:
1.Scatter plots:Create scatter plots ofyagainst each independent variable.
2. Residual plots:Plot residuals against predicted values. Residuals should berandomly
scattered around zero.

SECTION 6 Assumption 2 — Normality of Residuals
▷HOOK
The assumption:The error terms (residuals) are assumed to roughly follow anormal distri-
butionwith mean zero and varianceσ2.
Mean zeromeans the residuals are centered around zero.
Normal distributionmeans most residuals are small, and very large positive or negative
residuals are rare.
How to check:
Histogram of residuals— plot a histogram and check whether it resembles a bell-shaped curve
centered around zero.

SECTION 9 Assumption 5 — No Multicollinearity
▷HOOK
Multicollinearityoccurs when two or more independent variables in a regression model are
highly correlated. They exhibit a strong linear relationship, making it difficult to isolate the
individual effect of each variable on the dependent variable.
9.1 The Setup
Suppose we have two featuresX1 andX 2, and we want to predictY:
Y=β 0 +β 1X1 +β 2X2 +ε 1
Here,ε 1 represents the part ofYnot explained by the model.
IfX 1 andX 2 are highly correlated, then:
X1 =a 0 +a 1X2 +ε 2
If we substituteX1 into the equation forY, the model becomes:
Y≈C 0 +C 1X2 +ε 3
So, effectively,Ybecomes a linear function ofX 2 only.
