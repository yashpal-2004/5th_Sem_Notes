What Is DRAM?
'Dynamic' because the stored charge leaks away and must be constantly refreshed.
THE IDEA AT A GLANCE
DRAM stores each bit as CHARGE on a tiny capacitor:
charged = 1, empty = 0. 1T + 1C per bit cell — tiny
One transistor acts as a switch to access that capacitor.
That's it — just 1T + 1C per bit. 50–100 ns access — slower than SRAM
But capacitors leak. Within milliseconds the charge fades —
so DRAM must be refreshed thousands of times a second. Needs refresh every few milliseconds
Hence 'DYNAMIC'.
Very dense cheap per bit at scale

SRAM vs DRAM: Side by Side
The comparison you need cold for exams and interviews.
SRAM DRAM
Cell 6 transistors 1 transistor + 1 capacitor
Density Low High (~6× SRAM)
Speed Fast (a few ns) Slower (50–100 ns)
Refresh Not needed Every few ms
Read Non-destructive Destructive (write-back)
Cost / bit High Low
Used for Caches (L1/L2/L3) Main memory (RAM)
One sentence: SRAM trades density for speed; DRAM trades speed for density. Neither is 'better' — they're different tools.
